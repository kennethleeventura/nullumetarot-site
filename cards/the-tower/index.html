Rider–Waite–Smith · Pamela Colman Smith, 1909
Major Arcana · XVI

The Tower tarot card meaning

Upright, the Tower is a structure built on a false premise coming down — sudden, unchosen, and clarifying. Reversed, it is the same collapse delayed, resisted, or happening slowly on the inside. Either way, the card asks what was never as solid as it looked.

The image

Lightning strikes a tower on a peak. The crown is knocked from the top; two figures fall headfirst; flames come from the windows. Smith drew twenty-two small flames — the light falling with the people.

Upright

A sudden end to something that couldn't hold: a job, a belief, a version of a relationship. It rarely feels good. It is almost always true.

Reversed

Not "no collapse." The collapse is being held off, or it's happening quietly inside. Ask what you're propping up, and what it's costing to keep it standing.

In love, work, money and yourself

In love

The story you told about the relationship stops working. What's underneath is what you can build on.

In work

A restructure, a resignation, a plan that fails in public. Often the exit you wouldn't have chosen.

In money

An assumption about security turns out to be wrong. Look at what was load-bearing.

In yourself

A self-image cracks. Uncomfortable, and the start of something more honest.
