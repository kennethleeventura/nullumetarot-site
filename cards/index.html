How to use these card meanings

Every card page here covers the same ground, so you can compare cards quickly: what the picture shows, what the card means upright, what it means reversed, how it reads in love, work, money and in yourself, how the same card looks in older decks, its strongest pairings, and a few questions to sit with when it turns up.

Start with the image. The Rider–Waite–Smith deck, drawn by Pamela Colman Smith in 1909, puts a full scene on every card, and most of the meaning lives in the details: where a figure is looking, what the weather is doing, what has been left behind. Keywords are useful for remembering a card. The picture is what you actually read.

The Major Arcana and the Minor Arcana

A tarot deck has 78 cards. The 22 Major Arcana, numbered from 0 (The Fool) to XXI (The World), are the big forces and turning points: endings, beginnings, choices that change the direction of a life. The 56 Minor Arcana are the everyday weather, split into four suits: Wands for drive and appetite, Cups for feeling and connection, Swords for thought and conflict, and Pentacles for money, work and the body.

When a spread is full of Majors, the situation is bigger than one decision. When there are none, the outcome is in your hands. Pages for every Minor Arcana card are being written now and will appear here as they publish.

Upright, reversed, and what a card means beside another

A reversed card is rarely the opposite of its upright meaning. More often the energy is blocked, delayed, turned inward or overdone. The Tower reversed is not "no collapse"; it is a collapse being held off.

No card is read alone. Two cards side by side can reinforce each other, complement each other or pull in opposite directions, and that conversation is often the most useful part of a reading. Each card page lists its strongest pairings, and the pairing pages go deeper.
