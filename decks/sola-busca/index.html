Sola Busca: history, cards and how it reads.

The oldest complete tarot deck known, and the first with a scene on every pip card. Four centuries later, Pamela Colman Smith studied it before drawing the Rider–Waite–Smith minors.

Read with this deck

The deck's history

Engraved in northern Italy at the end of the fifteenth century, the Sola Busca takes its name from the Milanese family that owned it. Its trumps are not the familiar Fool-to-World figures but soldiers and heroes from ancient Rome.

What makes it matter to every modern reader is the pips. Where most early decks show a number of cups or swords, the Sola Busca draws a scene. That idea reached London in the twentieth century and shaped the deck most people read with today.

How NULLUME reads it

Your reader reads each card as its own image first, then by number and suit. The trumps are read through their named figures, with the familiar Major Arcana meaning offered as a second layer, never forced.
